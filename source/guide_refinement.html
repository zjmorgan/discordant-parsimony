Refinement, Validation, and Participation#

Covers how refinable correlations are fitted to measured intensities: the design matrix, choosing reciprocal-space positions, the least-squares problem and its uncertainties, validation on held-out data, and the participation scan that tests which atoms are correlated. Complete data are not required; what matters is a 3D sampling that determines the coefficients.

Design matrix#

The intensity is linear in the orbit coefficients, \(I = A \mathbf{c}\). Column \(k\) of design_matrix() is

\[A_k(\mathbf{Q}) = \sum_{p \in k} b_i b_j\, e^{-W_i - W_j}\, \cos(2\pi\,\mathbf{h}\cdot\mathbf{d}_p) \prod_a \operatorname{sinc}(\Delta h_a\, d_{p,a}),\]

with bin averaging included when bin widths are given. It is computed once per selection of positions and reused by every refinement and model comparison.

Selecting positions#

selection reduces a volume to the positions that carry independent diffuse information:

  • Bragg mask. Reflections allowed by the lattice centering (e.g. all-even or all-odd for F) are excluded within a radius in Å-1. The mask is a separate selection, so Bragg regions can be reintroduced for a later joint Bragg and diffuse refinement.

  • Laue asymmetric unit. Symmetrized data repeat each value across equivalent positions; keeping one representative per orbit (canonical()) avoids counting copies as independent observations.

  • Satellites and lines. Diffuse maxima are targeted like satellites: points \(\mathbf{G} + \mathbf{k}\) or lines \(\mathbf{G} + \mathbf{k} + t\mathbf{v}\) for allowed \(\mathbf{G}\) and the Laue star of \(\mathbf{k}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\).

  • Background sampling. A reproducible random fraction of the remaining positions, recorded with its seed.

Least squares#

With observed intensities \(y\), uncertainties \(\sigma\), a scale \(s\), and a smooth background basis \(B\),

\[y = s\,A(\mathbf{c}_0 + N\boldsymbol{\theta}) + B\boldsymbol{\beta} = s\,A\mathbf{c}_0 + (AN)\,\mathbf{u} + B\boldsymbol{\beta}, \qquad \mathbf{u} = s\boldsymbol{\theta}.\]

The problem is linear in \((s, \mathbf{u}, \boldsymbol{\beta})\) and is solved in one weighted step by refine(). The absolute scale comes from the fixed within-cluster terms in \(\mathbf{c}_0\). Uncertainties of every coefficient follow from the covariance of the solution by the delta method. The numerical rank and singular values are reported, and an optional ridge damping handles poorly covered selections. Tests confirm exact recovery from noise-free data and that the reported uncertainties match the scatter over repeated noisy refinements.

Validation on held-out data#

Refinements are scored on positions that were not used to fit them: the reduced \(\chi^2\) and the weighted R factor on the held-out set, playing the role of \(R_\text{free}\). A model with more parameters gains nothing unless it predicts unseen intensities better.

Participation scan#

participation_scan() answers which atoms are correlated. Candidate units are sets of site labels, usually one split-site cluster type each. For every combination of units, correlations involving units that are switched off are set to zero, while the exact within-cluster values are kept. Each model is refined on a training set and ranked by its held-out \(\chi^2\). Because the design matrix is shared, the scan costs one small singular-value decomposition per model.

Real-space maps from refined coefficients#

Refined coefficients give a model 3D-\(\Delta\)PDF through orbit_pair_terms() and delta_pdf_slab(). Unlike a direct Fourier inversion of the data, the map has no Bragg punch-and-fill, coverage gaps, or termination ripples, and needs only data that determine the coefficients. Individual coefficients can be less well determined than the map itself, because errors in combinations that share a real-space position cancel there.

Sampling in practice#

In the synthetic tests of Tb5Ru6Sn18: Which Atoms Participate?, a random 10% of the independent positions, or the streak lines plus a 2% background sample, recover the 3D-\(\Delta\)PDF and predict held-out intensities at the noise level. A single plane cannot determine 3D correlations, and the feature lines alone, without background positions, leave the problem rank deficient.
